Fund Manager Research Costs & Selection Evidence — Market Data

Manager selection is the most expensive research problem in investing, and the evidence on whether it pays is unusually blunt. Year after year, most active funds trail the benchmark they charge to beat — and the longer the horizon, the worse the record looks. That record is the reason research budgets, not just fund fees, belong in the cost conversation.

This page puts the selection evidence next to the research-spending evidence. The SPIVA scorecard counts how many funds beat their benchmarks. The CFA Institute surveys record what happened to research itself when regulation forced its price into the open. Both series point the same way: selection is hard, and the research behind it is getting thinner in exactly the corners where it might matter most.

Methodology

Included: SPIVA year-end 2024 scorecard figures as republished in public summaries, CFA Institute survey findings, and the published academic critique of SPIVA methodology. Excluded: any single manager's track record and any paywalled consultant database. SPIVA counts funds equally, not dollars — the Cremers critique is included precisely because that choice changes the answer, and readers should weigh both framings before drawing conclusions about manager selection.

Trend labels: “Baseline (October 2026) — trend tracking starts with the next refresh” means no prior-period figure from the same source was available this session, so no movement is claimed. Where a prior figure is shown, the trend compares the current figure with that prior figure from the same source.
